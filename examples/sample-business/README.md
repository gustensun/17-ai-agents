# Sample business: Nap Time Strength

A finished example of the team at work for one made-up business: the Brain from the setup interview, the first deliverable of all 17 agents, and the owner's ratings. Read it before you set up your own team, so you know what good output looks like.

Everything here is invented: the owner, the business, the customers, the numbers, the quotes and the links. Any match with a real person or business is a coincidence. Where a real page would go, you'll see a placeholder like [LINK].

## The business

Dana Park has been a personal trainer since 2015 and has two kids, ages 3 and 6. Nap Time Strength sells 20-minute home strength workouts to parents of kids under 5 who used to train.

- Free: the 20-Minute Week Planner, a printable PDF
- $27: the Nap Time Starter Kit, with a $9 Fridge Cards order bump and the $47 Strong Back Add-On
- $297, or 3 payments of $109: Back in 12, a 12-week group for 20 people, starting January 11, 2027
- $1,500: 12 weeks of 1:1 coaching

Dana has about 10 hours a week for the business, in nap times and two evenings. 3 of those hours go to reviewing the team's work.

## How the team was set up

1. **November 13, 2026: the interview.** The COO asked the [40 context questions](../../setup/40-context-questions.md), read 3 pieces Dana wrote herself and built [BRAIN.md](BRAIN.md).
2. **The team plan.** Dana's offer already sells, so the COO recommended the scaling team: COO, CMO, Analytics, Funnel, Copywriter, CRM, Automations and Support ([starter teams](../../setup/starter-teams.md#scaling)). For this example she installed all 17, so you can see every agent's work. In your own setup, start with 4 to 6.
3. **November 16 to 20: the training round.** Each agent did the first task on its card, up to 2 agents per sitting and two sittings a day. Dana rated every draft from 1 to 5 with one sentence on why, and the agent revised once ([setup protocol](../../setup/PROTOCOL.md#stage-6-training-round)). The ratings, and the 13 rules that came out of them, are in [taste-log.md](taste-log.md).

## How to read a deliverable

- **The header** says who made it, which Brain sections and skills it used, what's still a placeholder and the one decision the owner has to make. Every agent uses the same header ([brain/CORE.md](../../brain/CORE.md#formats)).
- **"Changed after your note"** shows what the first draft got wrong and what the agent changed.
- **Placeholders** in brackets, like [LINK], [CRM] or [ANSWER NEEDED: TV], mark what the agent couldn't know. Agents write a placeholder and never guess. Tool placeholders such as [CRM] or [FUNNEL SOFTWARE] stand for whichever tool you use.
- **"My setup"** is what the agent saved to its setup file, so its next task starts from there.

## Using it with your own team

- When a draft from your team looks thin, show the agent the matching file here and say "match this structure and level of detail, for my business." Dana's words, numbers and offers stay here.
- When you rate, copy the shape of Dana's notes in the [taste log](taste-log.md): a number, then one sentence that says what to change.
