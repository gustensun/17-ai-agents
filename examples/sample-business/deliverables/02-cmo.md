## The pitch, three ways

1. **The one line (Q14):** 12 weeks of 20-minute home workouts for parents who used to train, planned around nap time.
2. **The way you'd say it:** You used to train. Now you have small kids and 20 minutes that move around every day. The kit is 12 weeks of workouts that fit those 20 minutes, and a Plan B day for the days that don't go to plan.
3. **The story:** In January 2025 I set a timer for 20 minutes while my youngest napped. 12 weeks later I'd done 35 of 36 workouts. That plan is the Starter Kit.

## Winning Offer Formula

| Part | Element | From the Brain | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transformation | Who | Parents of kids under 5 who used to train | Strong |
| Transformation | Outcome | Strong again, training 3 times a week at home | Strong |
| Transformation | Problem | No free hour, days that change with naps, a lost routine | Strong, in their words |
| Transportation | Vehicle | The Nap Time Method: 3 workouts of 20 minutes, 5 moves, a Plan B day | Strong |
| Transportation | Source | A trainer since 2015 who did the 12 weeks as a parent of two | **Weakest.** The proof exists but isn't where people decide. The page uses 1 of the 3 testimonials you have permission for, and none of them sits next to a button |
| Transportation | Time | 12 weeks, 20 minutes, 3 times a week, workout 1 today | Strong |
| Transaction | Fee | $27, one payment | Fair, but shown with nothing to compare it to |
| Transaction | Risk | 30-day money-back guarantee, no questions asked | Strong, but only in the footer |
| Transaction | Priority | The honest cost of waiting. No deadline on the kit | Fine for evergreen |

The triangle:

- **Compulsion:** they want to be strong again, and 3 workouts of 20 minutes around naps shows them how. Present.
- **Confidence:** $27, 30 days to get the money back, and a trainer who did it as a parent. Missing at the button, where the proof and the guarantee are out of sight.
- **Convenience:** press play in the living room, and a missed day moves to Plan B. Present.

Wanting it but not buying usually means confidence is missing, which matches the 5.2% lead to kit rate.

## The value ladder and its gaps

Freebie (the planner PDF), then the kit ($27, with the $9 Fridge Cards and the $47 Strong Back Add-On), then Back in 12 ($297), then 1:1 coaching ($1,500).

1. **The front end makes less per buyer than planned.** The average order is $33.38 against $37: the bump goes to 31% of buyers against 35%, and the upsell to 8% against 15%.
2. **Nothing comes after week 12 while Back in 12 is closed.** It runs twice a year, so a buyer who finishes in March waits months. I've added "a next step for kit graduates" to the idea queue for after January.
3. **The freebie can't tailor anything.** The PDF doesn't know their nap times. The web tool fixes that later, and Coding writes the brief on Thursday.
4. **No path to 1:1 inside the kit.** Buyers never hear that 1:1 coaching exists. Funnel's plan adds it to the week-4 email.

## The math

Check it by hand:

- 13,100 views a week is about 56,300 a month, and 248 leads a week is about 1,066 a month (each x 4.3).
- At 5.2%, 1,066 leads buy 55 kits. At 6%, they buy 64. That's 9 more buyers a month from the same traffic.
- At 5.2%, the 60 kits on the Numbers card need 1,154 leads, which is about 61,000 views at today's 1.89%. That's 4,700 more views a month than now. At 6%, today's traffic is already enough.
