## Teardown: [COMPETITOR B]'s postpartum program

I opted in with the research email on November 18, as you allowed in setup, and bought nothing. Everything below is public or came to that email.

- **Promise:** "Bounce back in 8 weeks."
- **Price:** $79, one payment.
- **What's included:** 8 weeks of workouts of 30 to 40 minutes, 5 a week, a meal plan and a private group.
- **Proof:** before and after photos, and "10,000+ moms" with no source on the page.
- **Guarantee:** none stated.

The funnel, step by step:

1. An Instagram ad. [COMPETITOR B] has 7 active ads in the public ad library, and the oldest has run since August 2026. An ad that runs for months usually pays for itself, though that's a guess from the outside.
2. A quiz: "What's your postpartum body type?" with 6 questions.
3. The opt-in: the quiz result in exchange for an email.
4. The sales page, with a countdown timer that starts again every time the page reloads.
5. An order bump: a recipe book for $19.
6. An upsell: a core add-on for $49.
7. 6 emails in 8 days, with 3 different discount codes.

## Where Dana is different

1. **Strength you can count.** They sell a look in 8 weeks. You sell reps and weight that go up, which a parent can check at home.
2. **20 minutes against 30 to 40.** Their reviews complain about the length (Research found the same).
3. **The Plan B day.** Their plan has 5 workouts a week and nothing for a missed one.
4. **Real urgency only.** Their timer resets. Your Brain bans countdown timers, and Back in 12 has a real start date.
5. **Parents.** They write for moms only. About 3 in 10 on your list are dads.

Worth learning, without copying: the quiz collects more than an email before the offer. The planner web tool can do the same in our own way, by asking for nap times, which also makes the plan useful. I've passed this to Coding for Thursday's brief.
