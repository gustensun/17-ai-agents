What I started from: Q37 asks for "warm and real, like a living room in daylight", strong type, no pink and pastel, no stock photos. Q38 gives 40 photos from the June shoot and stills from the workout videos, and no logo yet. Kids appear only from behind or out of focus.

## Light, on a warm mid-tone

Your readers open the emails in nap time and after bedtime, on a phone, often in a dim room. A warm mid-tone background reads as daylight, matches Q37, and is softer at 9 pm than white. All three directions are light.

## A. The split stage

- **Why it fits:** parents trust a face. Half the screen holds one photo of you training in the living room, and the other half scrolls through the page.
- **The hero:** your photo on the left, mid-squat, a kid's toy in the corner. On the right: "Strong again in 20 minutes", the subhead and the button.
- **Palette:** Rose grey, green. Background #ebe5e3, text #2b2622, muted #5e5550, accent #0f7d45, button text #ffffff.
- **Type:** Hanken Grotesk 700 for headings, Hanken Grotesk 400 for text.
- **Motif:** the Plan B arrow, a curved arrow that moves a day forward. It shows up in the planner, the tracker and the button hover.
- **The risk:** the fixed half needs a strong photo for every section, and on phones the split stacks into a long page. Avoid it by picking 5 photos, one per section, before the build.

Contrast: text 12.01:1, muted 5.83:1, button 5.20:1. The green as small text on the background is 4.17:1, which passes for large text only, so small links use #0b6338 (5.90:1).

## B. The sentence is the layout (recommended)

- **Why it fits:** your promise is short and strong, and you have the photos. One sentence at poster size says it, with a strip of living-room photos sitting inside it where a word would be.
- **The hero:** "Strong again in [photo strip] 20 minutes." fills the first screen. The subhead and one button sit underneath.
- **Palette:** Sand and moss. Background #ddd6c6, surface #ece7dc, text #2a2a26, muted #5a564c, accent #4f6238, button text #ffffff.
- **Type:** Barlow Condensed 700 for the big sentence and headings, Barlow 400 for text.
- **Motif:** the 20-minute timer arc. It fills as the planner scrolls in, tracks progress in the tracker and draws the section dividers.
- **The risk:** a sentence at poster size can wrap badly on a phone. Avoid it by keeping the sentence to 6 words or fewer and giving the photo strip its own line under 600px.

Contrast: text 9.95:1, muted 5.05:1, button 6.70:1. On the surface colour: text 11.68:1, muted 5.93:1. The moss accent as text on the background is 4.63:1, so it passes for body text too.

## C. Colour chapters

- **Why it fits:** the 12 weeks have stages. Each section is one flat colour, floor to ceiling, held together by one font.
- **The hero:** a bottle-green screen with "12 weeks. 20 minutes. Nap time." in yellow-on-green blocks, and the button in yellow.
- **Palette:** Bottle ink, yellow. Background #f7f7f7, text #0d2a1c, muted #5c6b62, accent #f6e016 with #0d2a1c text.
- **Type:** Big Shoulders 800 for headings, Public Sans 400 for text.
- **Motif:** the Fridge Card. Each chapter is laid out like one card from the kit.
- **The risk:** flat bright colour plus condensed heavy type can feel like a bootcamp, which is the all-or-nothing fitness your customers left. Avoid it by keeping yellow to the button and one block per screen.

Contrast: text 14.36:1, muted 5.25:1, button text on yellow 11.42:1. Yellow on the light background is 1.26:1, so yellow is never used for text.

## Why B

1. It says the promise in their words before anything else. Research found "20 minutes" 31 times.
2. Your photos carry it without a photo for every section, which A needs.
3. The timer arc comes from the product itself, so it means something in the planner and the tracker.
4. It stays calm. C risks the bootcamp feel your customers left behind.
