# Content ideas: bad, good and great

For the Research, Creator and Content agents. A useful idea stands on proof that the topic already works, is aimed at the owner's customer, and is told through something only the owner can say.

The examples use the fictional business in [examples/sample-business](../examples/sample-business/README.md): a coach who helps parents of young kids get strong again with 20-minute home workouts. The links, numbers and quotes are made up.

## A video idea

> **Bad:** 5 tips to stay fit as a busy parent.

Anyone could make it. Nothing shows that people want it, and nothing gives a reason to watch this one over the hundred others.

> **Good:** I tried 20-minute workouts for 30 days as a parent of two.

A format that works (an experiment with a clear time frame), and specific to the customer. It still doesn't show proof that it works in this niche, and it doesn't lead anywhere.

> **Great:**
>
> IDEA: I trained 3 times a week for 12 weeks with a toddler at home. Here's what changed
> Proof it works: [LINK], 4.1x the channel's median, September. Format: 17, Extreme experiment
> Why it worked: one person, one hard limit, a clear before and after in the thumbnail
> Our version: the owner's own 12 weeks after their second child, with the numbers from the proof bank
> For: parents of kids under 5 who used to train   Belief it earns: 5, Myself, and 9, Time
> Pyramid: Empowering   CONTENT: N, New Discoveries   Next step: the free 20-Minute Week Planner

It keeps the format and the promise that already worked, and everything else comes from the owner.

## A research finding

> **Bad:** Parents are busy and want to get fit.

A summary. Nobody can write a headline from it.

> **Good:** Parents say they don't have time to work out. Source: 3 forum threads, linked.

True and sourced, but it's still in the researcher's words.

> **Great:** "I haven't been to the gym since my second kid was born. I don't need a program, I need 20 minutes nobody can take from me." Source: [LINK], posted [DATE], 212 replies. Avatar grid box: external problem. Use it as the hook for the next video and a headline test on the opt-in page.

The customer's exact words, with the source, the box it fills in the avatar grid and what to do with it.

## A creator report item

> **Bad:** Make a video like this one: [LINK].

> **Great:** A meal-prep creator's "3 meals I cook every Sunday" got 5.2x their median ([LINK], October). Format: 14, Steal this. The thumbnail shows the 3 containers and nothing else. Our version: "The 3 workouts I do when I only have 20 minutes", filmed in the owner's living room, ending on the free planner.

It names what worked (the format and the packaging), why it worked, and how the owner's version is different. Nothing is copied from the original video.

## The idea card

Every idea the three agents hand over uses this card.

```
IDEA: [TITLE IN THE OWNER'S WORDS]
Proof it works: [LINK], [MULTIPLE]x the channel's median, [MONTH]. Format: [NUMBER AND NAME]
Why it worked: [PROMISE, HOOK, PACKAGING]
Our version: [THE STORY, EXAMPLE, OPINION OR PROOF THAT MAKES IT THE OWNER'S]
For: [THE CUSTOMER FROM BRAIN SECTION 3]   Belief it earns: [NUMBER AND NAME]
Pyramid: [LEVEL]   CONTENT: [LETTER AND TYPE]   Next step: [FREEBIE, OFFER OR NEXT VIDEO]
```

## Checks

- It stands on proof: an outlier, a question customers keep asking, or research with a link.
- It's for the owner's customer, even when the outlier came from a different audience.
- It has something only the owner could say: their story, their numbers or one of their strong opinions (Q16).
- It ends with one next step.
- No title, script or thumbnail is copied. Formats and promises are fair to learn from.
- Money claims follow house rule 11 in [brain/CORE.md](../brain/CORE.md).

The format numbers and names come from [skills/outlier-research](../skills/outlier-research/SKILL.md), and the pyramid and CONTENT letters from [skills/content-system](../skills/content-system/SKILL.md).
