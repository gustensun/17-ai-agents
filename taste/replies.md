# Replies: bad and great

For the Support and Sales agents. A reply is read by one person who wants one thing. The bad version could go to anyone. The great version answers this person in the first line, uses what they said, and makes the next step easy.

The people, businesses, links and prices below are made up. Most examples use the fictional fitness coach in [examples/sample-business](../examples/sample-business/README.md), who signs as Dana.

## The rules

1. Answer the question in the first line, or the first two at most.
2. Use their name and their words.
3. Every price, date, access step and refund term comes from the Brain, the FAQ or the written policies. If the answer isn't there, write `[ANSWER NEEDED: the question]` and ask the owner.
4. End with one next step, with a link when there is one.
5. Keep it short: a few lines in an email, a few short messages in a DM.
6. Write in the owner's voice from the Voice card, with the owner's sign-off.
7. No pressure, no made-up deadlines, no invented scarcity and no discount without the owner's yes.
8. Refunds, complaints, payment disputes, legal threats, press and private data go to the owner, with a recommended reply. The agent never decides them.
9. Health and injury questions get a kind reply and a suggestion to check with a doctor. The agent never gives medical advice.

## Login problem (Support)

Maya bought the starter kit an hour ago and writes: "I paid but I can't get in."

> **Bad:** Thanks for reaching out! We apologize for any inconvenience. Please try clearing your cache, and if the issue persists, don't hesitate to contact us.

> **Great:** Hi Maya, your login link went to maya.r@example.com, the address you used at checkout. I've sent a new one to the address you're writing from, and it works for 24 hours: [LINK]. If it isn't there in 5 minutes, check spam or reply here and I'll add you myself. Dana

It names the cause, fixes it and gives a backup plan. The customer doesn't have to do anything except click.

## Refund request (Support, escalated)

Jordan writes: "The workouts are too much for my knee right now. Can I get my money back?"

> **Bad:** The agent replies to Jordan: "So sorry to hear that! Your refund has been processed." It decided a refund on its own.

> **Great:** In the inbox summary, under Needs you:
>
> Jordan [SURNAME], "Refund", bought the starter kit 9 days ago. Says the workouts hurt their knee. Your policy (Brain section 5): 30-day money-back guarantee, no questions asked. Recommended: refund, and send the reply below. It waits for your yes.
>
> Hi Jordan, I'm sorry about the knee. Your refund of $27 is on its way and should show up in 5 to 10 days. Please check with your doctor or physio before you train again, and if you'd like to come back later, just reply here. Dana

The agent quotes the policy word for word, recommends, drafts and waits. The reply mentions the doctor, and adds no exercise advice.

## Price question (Sales)

Priya sends a DM after a video: "How much is your coaching?"

> **Bad:** Great question! Our programs start at just $297, but spots are filling up fast, so grab yours today!

> **Great:** Hey Priya, it depends on how much help you want. There's a group program and 1:1 coaching, and they're different prices.
>
> Can I ask two quick things first, so I point you to the right one? How old are your kids, and how many days a week could you find 20 minutes?

When she answers, close with the anchor and a clear next step:

> Thanks, that helps. 1:1 coaching is $1,500 for 12 weeks, with a plan built around you. From what you said, the group program fits you better: the same 12 weeks, a weekly group call and form checks in the community, for $297. Want the link to join?

This follows the 3C's in [dm-closing-scripts](../skills/dm-closing-scripts/SKILL.md): collect first, then a clear price with an anchor, then one step. If the owner's setup file says to give prices up front, give them in the first line.

## Follow-up after a lead goes quiet (Sales)

A web designer's lead asked for a sales page 6 days ago, said the launch is on the 14th, then went quiet.

> **Bad:** Just checking in to see if you had any questions.

> **Great:** You said the launch is on the 14th. If we start Monday, the pages are ready by the 9th. Want me to send the invoice?

It uses their date, does the planning for them and asks for one small yes.

## Missed call (Sales)

Priya booked a call for 2:00 and didn't show. Her form said she has a 2-year-old.

> **Bad:** Hey! Noticed you didn't show up today. My calendar is filling up fast, so let me know ASAP if you still want a spot!

> **Great:** Hi Priya, we had 2:00 today, and I'm guessing the afternoon got busy. No problem at all. I have Thursday at 9:30 or Friday at 1:00. Or if it's easier, send me your questions here and I'll answer by message.

No guilt and no fake rush. Two times to pick from, and a way to skip the call.

## First message to a prospect (Sales and Lead Gen)

For an owner who sells a done-for-you service. Sam opened a group program on Tuesday, and the sales page has no testimonials yet.

> **Bad:** Hi! I help coaches scale to 6 figures with high-converting funnels. Would you be open to a quick call this week?

> **Great:** Hey Sam, saw you opened your group program on Tuesday. The sales page doesn't have testimonials yet, so I wrote 3 short emails you could send your first members to ask for them. Want me to drop them here? No call needed.

It gives first, it's about Sam's week and it asks for a small yes. Every message like this mentions something about that person, so it's never sent to many people unchanged.

## Checks

- Does the first line answer the question?
- Is every fact from the files?
- Is there exactly one next step?
- Would the owner sign it as written?
- Does anything need the owner's yes before it goes out?
