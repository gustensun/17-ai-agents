# Copy: bad, good and great

The same offer written three ways: a done-for-you content and email service for coaches, consultants and service providers. The three versions come from a copywriting lesson. Read the notes on each, then use the pattern at the end on every hero, ad and email you write.

## Example A: how not to write copy

> Attention: If you're looking to get more customers for your business, then I have the perfect solution for you...
>
> **We will personally hunt your leads and get you 20-30 leads per month so you can close more deals, without paying for expensive in-house marketers - or you don't pay us anything.**
>
> Leveraging our proprietary lead-getting ad solution optimized to acquire inbound leads for you while you sleep, so you can focus on what you love doing the most, without the price tag of in-house team.
>
> Button: YES, I WANT THIS SOLUTION NOW!

Why it fails:

- **Nobody is called out.** "Your business" fits every business, so no reader feels it was written for them.
- **It's about the seller.** The headline starts with "We will", and the reader has to work out what they get.
- **It promises the wrong thing.** Leads still have to be closed. The buyer wants clients.
- **The reader can't picture it.** "Proprietary lead-getting ad solution optimized to acquire inbound leads" hides what the service does.
- **Stock phrases.** "While you sleep" and "focus on what you love doing the most" are on thousands of pages.
- **The button shouts and says nothing.** Capitals and an exclamation mark, and the reader still doesn't know what happens after the click.
- **The best part is buried.** "You don't pay us anything" sits at the end of a 30-word headline.

## Example B: good copy

> Attention: Coaches, Consultants and Service Providers wanting to scale...
>
> **What if you could get 20-30 new ready-to-buy clients, hand delivered to you every single month, without paying a single dollar upfront...**
>
> Would you take me up on that offer? We'll research, write, edit and post all your emails and content pieces for you completely hands-off, and just deliver the clients to you so you can close them with ease.
>
> Button: Book a free 15 minute call
>
> Link: or click here to message me directly for any questions you have.

Why it works:

- **The first line picks the reader:** three kinds of business and the goal they share.
- **The headline is about the reader** and promises what they want: clients who are ready to buy.
- **The risk is gone:** "without paying a single dollar upfront".
- **The subhead shows the work** in four plain verbs: research, write, edit, post.
- **The button says what happens and what it costs:** a call, free, 15 minutes.
- **There's a softer second step:** a text link for people who'd rather send a message than book a call.

## Example C: great copy

> THE #1 GHOSTWRITING SERVICE TO GET YOU CLIENTS
>
> **Unlimited Content & Emails Done-For-You, Completely Hands-Off Without You Lifting a Finger**
>
> Get 20-30 new clients every month without working harder. We handle everything, from research and writing to editing and posting your emails and content. You just close the deals, and you keep 90% of the profits.
>
> Button: Get a free 15 minute call
>
> Link: or click here to message me directly for any questions you have.

What it adds to B:

- **It names the category in the first line.** The reader knows it's a ghostwriting service within a second.
- **The headline is the offer itself:** unlimited content and emails, done for them, with no effort on their side.
- **The subhead carries three things:** the outcome (new clients every month), the work (research, writing, editing, posting) and the deal (they close, they keep 90% of the profits).
- **"Get" beats "Book".** Getting a call sounds like receiving something. Booking a call sounds like a chore.
- **Every line is short enough to read on a phone in one pass.**

## Before you ship lines like C

Copy the structure. The claims need proof first (house rule 1 in [brain/CORE.md](../brain/CORE.md)).

- **"#1"** is a ranking. Use it only when the proof bank backs it, and say who ranked it. Otherwise write "The ghostwriting service that gets you clients."
- **"20-30 new clients every month"** is a result. It needs proof in Brain section 7. Until then, write `[RESULT: X new clients a month, for Y customers]`.
- **"Keep 90% of the profits"** has to match the real price model exactly.

## The pattern

1. **First line:** call out the reader, or name the category.
2. **Headline:** the result or the offer, in the reader's words, with the effort or the risk removed.
3. **Subhead:** the work in plain verbs, and the deal.
4. **Button:** a verb, what they get and what it costs them ("Get a free 15 minute call").
5. **A text link** for readers who aren't ready for the button.
6. **Proof** within one screen of the button (check B10 in [conversion-design](../skills/conversion-design/SKILL.md)).

## Same pattern, other businesses

> **Bad:** Take your fitness to the next level with our revolutionary program!
>
> **Great:** Three 20-minute home workouts a week, planned around nap time. For parents who keep starting over every January.

> **Bad:** Streamline your finances with cutting-edge bookkeeping solutions.
>
> **Great:** Your books closed by the 5th of every month, so you know your profit before you pay yourself.

> **Bad:** Unlock your creative potential today.
>
> **Great:** Sell your first print in [TIME], with the camera you already own. [PROOF: students who did it]

## Button labels

| Weak | Why | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Submit | Says what the form does, and nothing about what they get | Send me the checklist |
| Learn more | No promise and no next step | See the 3 steps |
| Click here | Names the action, and skips the reward | Get instant access |
| Sign up | Sounds like work | Save my seat |
