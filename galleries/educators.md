# Educators gallery: 1,550 paid communities

The top 50 paid communities in each of 31 categories on a community platform's public leaderboards, 1,550 groups in all, collected November 16, 2026. Use it when you design or price a membership, a paid community or a group program: what goes in the offer stack, in what order, and what price the category supports. It shows what the market does. The owner's offer still comes from the Brain.

The live version, with covers, offer stacks and filters: [educators gallery](https://educators-gallery.vibepreview.app/). The live site names the groups and their owners. This file leaves out names, owners and each group's revenue, so you can read it without opening the site. Every number here was recomputed from the gallery's data for this file, so a few differ from the live site's offer stack panel ([how the numbers were made](#how-the-numbers-were-made)).

## Rules

1. Use the patterns. Never copy a group's name, cover, tagline, offer stack or price. Write the owner's offer from the Brain, in the owner's voice.
2. Revenue, growth and member counts are the leaderboards' estimates. The group owners didn't report them. Use them to compare patterns, and never quote them in the owner's content.
3. These are patterns, and they don't prove cause. A group at the top may have a bigger audience, a head start or a hotter niche. Use them to decide what to test first.
4. Every timeframe, guarantee, price rise and seat limit in the owner's offer must be true, as [winning-offer-formula](../skills/winning-offer-formula/SKILL.md) says. Income and result claims follow house rule 11 in [brain/CORE.md](../brain/CORE.md).

## How to use it

1. Read Brain section 3 (the customer) and section 5 (the offer and the value ladder).
2. Find the owner's category in [Category benchmarks](#category-benchmarks). Note the median price and how often groups offer a guarantee, a free trial, a free tier or a price rise.
3. Build the stack in the order below: main offer, bonuses, urgency, then risk reversal. Begin with [the core four](#start-with-the-core-four).
4. Open the live gallery, pick the category, and read 5 to 10 offer stacks sorted by Top MRR and 5 to 10 sorted by Fastest growing. Note what they share.
5. Shape the offer with [winning-offer-formula](../skills/winning-offer-formula/SKILL.md) and check the price against the owner's goal with [seven-figure-math](../skills/seven-figure-math/SKILL.md). Tell the owner which parts you used, which you left out and what to test first.

## What the top 10 do differently

Each category's top 10 compared with its ranks 41 to 50: 310 groups on each side.

- **A higher price.** The median listed price is $59 a month in the top 10 and $47 in ranks 41 to 50.
- **More parts in the stack.** The top 10 use 2.6 of the 16 parts below on average. Ranks 41 to 50 use 2.1.
- **A money-back guarantee.** 25% offer one, against 12%. It's the biggest gap in the data.
- **Live calls.** 42% mention live calls on a set rhythm, against 30%.

## The offer stack

16 parts, in the order to present them. "Top 10 vs ranks 41 to 50" is the share of groups in each slice that use the part. "With vs without" compares medians across all 1,550 groups. Example lines use brackets for the owner's real details.

### Main offer: what they are buying

1. **A specific outcome and a timeframe.** No more common at the top (8% vs 9%). Groups that name one show a higher median est. MRR ($10.3K vs $8.4K) and faster growth (median 30-day change +$269 vs +$3).
   - Example: "Get [specific result] in [timeframe]."
2. **A step-by-step roadmap.** A little more common at the top (27% vs 25%). Present it as a path from where they are to the result, with a deadline.
   - Example: "A week-by-week roadmap from [starting point] to [result]."
3. **Live calls on a set rhythm.** 42% vs 30%. The most common part in the top 10, and one of the two biggest gaps.
   - Example: "[Number] live calls a week, with hot seats and Q&A."
4. **Direct access to the owner.** 11% vs 6%. Promise only the access the owner can keep up.
   - Example: "I answer every post myself within [number] hours."
5. **Community framed as identity.** 27% vs 20%. Talk about who members become, and who else is in the room.
   - Example: "The community for [who they want to become]."
6. **Proof and member wins.** No clear gap in this data (12% vs 12%). Use only real results from the proof bank (house rule 11).
   - Example: "[Number] members [reached a result]. See the wins."

### Bonuses: what makes it feel like more than the price

7. **Templates, scripts and swipe files.** About the same share at both ends (14% vs 16%), and one of the parts most linked to growth: median 30-day change +$252 vs +$3.
   - Example: "[Number] editable templates and scripts."
8. **Tools, software or perks with a stated value.** Rare (2.4% of groups) and more common at the top (2.9% vs 0.6%). List only discounts the owner has in writing.
   - Example: "[Dollar value] in tool discounts included."
9. **Done-for-you or setup help.** Rare (3.1% of groups), with the highest median est. MRR of any part: $15.4K vs $8.4K.
   - Example: "An onboarding call where we set it up with you."
10. **Daily touchpoints.** 15% vs 9%. Daily contact gives members a reason to open the group.
   - Example: "A daily Q&A thread, answered by me."
11. **Guest experts and interviews.** No clear gap in this data (3.5% vs 3.5%). Use it when the guests are real and booked.
   - Example: "Two guest expert sessions every month."
12. **Challenges, prizes and gamification.** 12% vs 7%.
   - Example: "A 30-day challenge every month, with prizes for the top 3."
13. **Leads, a job board or deal flow.** 10% vs 7%. Most common in careers, real estate, trades, sales and marketing groups.
   - Example: "Weekly [client leads, job posts or deals] for members."

### Urgency: why join now

14. **A price rise with a founding price lock.** About as common at the top as lower down (16% vs 15%), and the part most linked to growth: median 30-day change +$299 vs $0. The rise has to be real and on a date or a member count you state.
   - Example: "[Price] a month now, [higher price] at [number] members. Your rate stays the same while you stay."

### Risk reversal: why it is safe to say yes

15. **A free trial or a free tier as the way in.** A free trial is a little more common at the top (21% vs 18%), and groups with one grow faster (median 30-day change +$56 vs $0) at a lower median est. MRR ($7.7K vs $9.0K). A free tier is less common at the top (15% vs 18%). Use either one to bring people in, then sell the paid level.
   - Example: "Try it free for 7 days."
16. **A money-back guarantee, tied to a result when you can.** The biggest gap in the data: 25% of top-10 groups vs 12% of ranks 41 to 50. Median est. MRR $13.5K with one, $7.8K without. Spell out the terms.
   - Example: "Do the work, get [result] in [timeframe], or your money back."

## Start with the core four

1. A step-by-step roadmap, so the program has a path.
2. Live calls on a set rhythm (42% vs 30%).
3. Direct access to the owner (11% vs 6%).
4. A money-back guarantee (25% vs 12%).

Live calls and the guarantee show the biggest gaps in the data. The next biggest are community framed as identity (27% vs 20%) and daily touchpoints (15% vs 9%). Then add templates and a real price rise, two of the parts most linked to growth.

## What did not move the needle

- **Certificates.** Rare (19 groups) and a little less common at the top (1.3% vs 1.9%).
- **In-person events.** 2.3% vs 2.6%, with a lower median est. MRR ($7.6K vs $8.6K).
- **"Limited spots" wording.** Rare (15 groups) and just as common in ranks 41 to 50 as in the top 10 (1% vs 1%). A seat limit has to be real.
- **A free tier.** Less common at the top (15% vs 18%), with a lower median est. MRR ($6.3K vs $9.2K).

## How the numbers were made

- **Pool.** The top 50 paid communities by estimated monthly revenue in each of 31 categories, from a community platform's public leaderboards, collected November 16, 2026.
- **Estimates.** Monthly revenue (MRR), the 30-day change in it and member counts are the leaderboards' estimates.
- **Prices.** Read from each group's public page. Median prices use monthly prices in US dollars only, which leaves out yearly, one-time, free and unlisted groups and 2 groups priced in pounds.
- **Guarantee, free trial, free tier and price rise.** Flags in the gallery's data for each group.
- **The other parts.** Keyword matches in each group's tagline and the first lines of its offer stack. That text is cut at about 300 characters, so the shares undercount. Live calls, for example, counts mentions of live calls, Q&As, hot seats, office hours and group coaching.
- **Medians.** "With vs without" compares all 1,550 groups that use a part with all that don't, across categories. Category size and price mix sit inside those numbers.
- **The live site.** Its offer stack panel came from an earlier keyword pass, and several of its shares run higher. Where the two passes point different ways, this file goes with the recomputed numbers and says so in that part's line.
