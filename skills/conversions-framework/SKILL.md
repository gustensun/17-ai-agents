# The CONVERSIONS framework

Eleven checks, one per letter. Use it as a pre-launch audit and as the first step when a page underperforms. The 17P's in [17p-sections](../17p-sections/SKILL.md) build the page. CONVERSIONS checks that each part does its job.

| Letter | Check | Its job | Audit question | Where it usually lives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C | Clear Outcome | Make them interested to stay | Is the promise clear on the first screen? | Promise, hero |
| O | Opportunity Selling | Get them to visualize their future | Does it sell the future instead of the features? | Promise, Positioning of Offer |
| N | New Method | Show them a new, better way | Does it show why this way is different from what they tried? | Personal Epiphany, Process to Outcome |
| V | Value Proposition | Describe how it'll benefit them | Does every feature say what changes in their life? | Product Breakdown, Positioning of Offer |
| E | Empathy | Make them feel understood | Would the buyer say "that's me" in the first two sections? | Problem, Pain Points |
| R | Results | Let results do the talking | Is the owner's own proof clear and believable? | Proof of Results |
| S | Social Proof | Show others loving it | Do other people vouch for it, close to the claims? | Proof from Others, Project Spotlight |
| I | Irresistible Offer | Make it hard to not buy it | Is the stack clear and the price anchored with real values? | Price Anchor, Product Bonuses, Pricing Tables |
| O | Objection Handling | Acknowledge and calm their fears | Are the top objections from the Brain answered? | Pushback Reflex, FAQ, Perfect Persona |
| N | Next Steps | Invite them with a call to action | Is the next step obvious, repeated and the same everywhere? | Promotional Bar, every button |
| S | Scarcity | Urgency | Is the urgency real? Remove anything fake. | Priority |

## The proof mix

Strong pages mix four kinds of proof. Check which are missing.

1. **Your results:** what the owner did for themselves.
2. **Your positioning:** data or a story that shows why the owner sees what others miss.
3. **Others' results:** testimonials and case studies.
4. **Outside signals:** engagement, shares, press, reviews on other platforms, community size.

Proof comes only from the Brain (section 7). A missing type becomes a `[PROOF: what kind]` slot and a line in the report, never an invention.

## Run the audit

1. Read the page top to bottom as the avatar from the Brain, arriving from the real traffic source for the first time, a bit skeptical and busy.
2. Score each letter from 1 to 5 and write the fix for anything under 4.
3. Check the proof mix and list the missing types.
4. Note the first moment you felt doubt, the question the page never answered and where you would have left.
5. Pick the three fixes that will make the biggest difference. Start with the earliest letter that scored lowest: a buyer who isn't interested at C never reaches the offer at I.

## Split tests after launch

Test in this order: headlines and wording first, the hero section second, then the layout, page length, video or no video, writing style, section order, where the proof sits and the graphic style.

- One change per test, so you know what caused the result.
- Write the hypothesis before the test: "Changing [X] to [Y] will raise [metric] because [reason from the audit]."
- Decide the stopping rule before you start: a fixed number of visitors per version, or a fixed number of days that covers full weeks. Low traffic means bigger changes, tested longer.
- Log every test and its result in `my-business/log.md`, and the winner in the taste log, so every agent learns from it.

```
TEST PLAN: [page]   Traffic: [visitors per week]   Current rate: [%]
1. [what changes] | Hypothesis: [...] | Stop at: [visitors or days]
2. ...
```
