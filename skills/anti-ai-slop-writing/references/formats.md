# Format notes

Read only the section that matches the piece. These add to the drafting rules in `SKILL.md`.

## Social and personal-brand posts

- Start with what happened or what the owner thinks. The first line should still make sense if the rest were cut off.
- Write in paragraphs of two to four sentences. A post made of one-line paragraphs reads as a template.
- A small event stays small. Report it, say what the owner changed because of it, and leave the lesson at that size.
- Vulnerability has to come from the owner: the actual mistake, what it cost, what is still unresolved. If they gave none, write a useful post without it.
- A post may be observational, funny, unfinished, or useful with no offer attached.
- End on the last fact or the owner's decision. Ask a closing question only when the owner wants specific answers and will read them.
- Hashtags and emoji only when the owner asks for them.

## Email, DMs and support replies

- Give the reason for writing in the first two sentences.
- Match the existing relationship and the sender's usual formality. A message to a client of five years and a first cold email are different documents.
- One ask per message, with what the reader should do and by when.
- A routine logistical message needs no added warmth. Be polite by being short and clear.
- Cold outreach: mention one thing that is true only of this recipient, then the ask. Skip the compliment paragraph.
- Support replies: answer the question first, then the steps, then what happens if the steps fail.
- Sign off the way the owner does.

## Sales pages, ads and funnels

- Keep every conversion section the page already has (headline, problem, mechanism, proof, offer, guarantee, FAQ, call to action). This skill edits sentences. The structure comes from the 17P sections and the funnel skills.
- Describe the buyer's situation the way the buyer would recognize it on a Tuesday afternoon: the tab they have open, the number that worries them, the thing they already tried.
- Tie every benefit to a feature, a mechanism, a proof point or a scenario. A benefit with none of these gets cut or gets a placeholder.
- Lead with the owner's strongest true claim and let the other claims be smaller. A page where every claim is maximal is believed nowhere.
- Use the objections real buyers raise, in their words when the Brain has them. Say who should not buy.
- Price, guarantee terms, deadlines and bonus values must come from the Brain. Use placeholders otherwise.
- The call to action says what happens after the click: what they get, when, and what it costs.
- Urgency is stated only when it's real, with the actual date or number.

## Articles, newsletters and search content

- Answer the query in the first paragraph, then expand.
- Add what competing pages can't have: the owner's firsthand experience, a calculation, a screenshot, a sourced figure, a position.
- Keep a definition, FAQ, trends section or conclusion only when a reader needs it. Don't copy the average outline of the top results.
- Headings state the claim or the question the section answers, in sentence case.
- Required keywords stay, placed where they read naturally. Pass them to the scan with `--keep`.
- Link each sourced claim to its source in the sentence that makes it.

## Product descriptions

- Describe materials, dimensions, weight, behavior in use, compatibility, care and limits.
- Include the detail a buyer can't get from a photo: texture, sound, heat, how it sits in the hand.
- Say who it suits and who should pick something else.
- Verifiable detail creates the desire. Praise words don't.

## Case studies and testimonials

- Cover the starting point, what was done, the time frame, the result and the main caveat, as far as the Brain supplies them.
- Give results with their unit and period: 31 booked calls in 6 weeks, up from 9.
- Include what didn't work or took longer than planned when the owner mentions it. A story with no friction reads as invented.
- Customer quotes are word for word only. Keep rough or plain phrasing. Never write a quote for a customer, and never tidy one beyond fixing a typo.

## Teaching material

- Define a term only if this reader needs the definition.
- Explain causes, constraints, and the cases where the method fails.
- Develop one example fully before adding another.
- Keep the owner's framework names and steps exactly. Don't coin new labels or acronyms for ideas the owner hasn't named.
- Write steps as instructions with the expected result of each, so the learner knows it worked.

## Video and spoken scripts

- Write for the ear. Read every line aloud before keeping it.
- Spoken language repeats, uses fragments and asks real questions. Treat the scan's rhythm findings (fragments, repeated openers, short paragraphs) as hints here, and its HARD findings as binding.
- One idea per breath. If a sentence needs a second breath, split it.
- Say numbers the way a person says them, and round them unless precision is the point.
- The opening line states what the viewer will get or shows the result. Cut any greeting that delays it.

## Writing about AI

- Make the claim the evidence in front of you supports: what the tool did, on what input, how long it took, where it failed.
- Name the model, version and date, since behavior changes between releases.
- Skip the reflex conclusion about people and machines. If the piece needs a view on that, argue it from the specific case.
