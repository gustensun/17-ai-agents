# The Winning Ad Formula

Twenty conversion boosters. A long ad for a low-ticket offer uses most of them, in roughly this order. A short ad keeps the starred ones. Every booster needs a real fact behind it from the Brain: a missing fact becomes a placeholder, never an invention.

| # | Booster | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook \* | The opener. Grabs attention, stops the scroll and calls out the problem, the promise or the opportunity. Bold, specific, curious. |
| 2 | Credibility \* | Why they should listen to you: years in the game, client results, credentials, a track record. |
| 3 | Price Anchor | Sets a high perceived value first, then shows the lower price. The contrast makes the offer feel like a steal. |
| 4 | Shortcut \* | The easy button: how the offer saves time, effort or mistakes. Done for you, or plug and play. |
| 5 | Proof \* | Social proof, testimonials or numbers (customers, users, results) that back up the claims. |
| 6 | Open Loop | Curiosity about something they don't know yet (a bonus, a framework, a secret) so they keep reading. |
| 7 | Convenience | How easy it is to use, in a few steps, without extra tools, skills or stress. |
| 8 | Analogy | A metaphor (like building with blocks, or a doctor's appointment) that makes the system easy to picture. |
| 9 | Logical Process | The steps in a clear order. Removes doubt and shows the system works. |
| 10 | Framework | The method as a repeatable, named system instead of random tactics. A named framework builds trust. |
| 11 | Value Anchor | The real effort, investment or money that went into building it. Makes it feel worth more than the price. |
| 12 | Wow Bundle Offer Stack \* | Bonuses, extras and tools, each one stacking value and killing one objection. |
| 13 | CTA \* | The direct instruction: click, buy, download. Clear and unavoidable. |
| 14 | Urgency | Real time pressure: the price goes up, bonuses disappear, doors close. |
| 15 | Next Steps | Exactly what happens after they buy: instant access, email delivery, login details. Removes friction. |
| 16 | Final CTA | The call to action again at the end, after the stack and the objections. |
| 17 | Desired Outcome | What life looks like after they buy: more clients, less stress, more time, more income. |
| 18 | Domain | Your own branded link instead of a messy URL. Looks more legit. |
| 19 | Strong CTA | Urgency again, in plain direct words, often paired with the guarantee. |
| 20 | Guarantee \* | Risk reversal: a money-back guarantee, a free trial or a refund promise. |

## The long-ad skeleton

Fill it from the Brain. Brackets stay brackets until the owner gives the real fact.

```
[HOOK: price anchor or outcome. Example shape: "[EXPENSIVE WAY] costs [REAL PRICE]. You don't have to pay that."]
[CREDIBILITY: one line of real track record]
[PRICE ANCHOR + SHORTCUT: "Now you get [WHAT] for a fraction of that, [READY TO USE]."]
[PROOF: "[REAL NUMBER] people already use it."]
[OPEN LOOP: "And they didn't get the [NUMBER] bonuses in this one."]
[CONVENIENCE + LOGICAL PROCESS:
All you have to do is:
1) [STEP]
2) [STEP]
3) [STEP]]
[ANALOGY: "It's like [FAMILIAR THING]."]
[FRAMEWORK + VALUE ANCHOR: "Follow [NAMED METHOD]. I spent [REAL TIME OR MONEY] building it so you can [SHORTCUT]."]
[OFFER STACK: "Here's everything you get for [PRICE] with a [GUARANTEE]:" then one line per item, each with what it does for them]
[CTA + DOMAIN: "Get it here: [YOUR DOMAIN]/[PATH]"]
[URGENCY: only a real one, with the date or the limit]
[NEXT STEPS: "Once you buy, everything lands in your inbox within [TIME]."]
[FINAL CTA + DOMAIN]
```

A short ad (under 80 words): Hook, Credibility, Shortcut, Proof, the stack in one line, CTA, Guarantee.

## How to write an ad set

1. Pick the angle. Use the strongest pain or desire lines from the avatar grid (Brain section 3) and the hooks that worked in [outlier-research](../outlier-research/SKILL.md). One angle per ad.
2. Write three hooks per angle: one gain, one pain, one curiosity. The hook usually moves the numbers most, so test hooks before anything else.
3. Build the body with the skeleton. Cut any booster you can't back with a fact.
4. Write the matching headline and the first line of the landing page, so the click lands on the same promise.
5. For video: the hook in the first two seconds, said and shown on screen, one idea per shot, the CTA said out loud.
6. Run the scanner from [anti-ai-slop-writing](../anti-ai-slop-writing/SKILL.md). Ads get extra hype checks: no fake scarcity, no income claims without proof and a disclaimer.
7. Send the set to the owner. Ads always need approval before they run, and every spend needs a yes.

```
AD SET: [offer]   Angle: [one line]   Placement: [platform, format]
Hook A (gain): ...
Hook B (pain): ...
Hook C (curiosity): ...
Body: [skeleton, filled]
Headline: ...   Landing page first line: ...
Boosters used: [numbers]   Placeholders: [list]
Test: hooks first, same body, [budget or "owner decides"]
```

## When ads get clicks but no sales

The ad works and the page doesn't. Check that the page's first screen repeats the ad's promise in the same words, then run [conversions-framework](../conversions-framework/SKILL.md) on the page. When ads get no clicks, test new hooks before you touch the offer.
