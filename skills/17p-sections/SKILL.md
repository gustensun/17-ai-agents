# The 17P's

Seventeen sections that show up again and again in funnels that convert, plus the FAQ and footer. They are the sections and the sequence of a page. A long sales page uses most of them. An opt-in page uses a few. Every section you cut should be a decision: list the sections you left out and why.

The copy comes first and the design follows it. Each section here becomes one section in [conversion-design](../conversion-design/SKILL.md).

## The seventeen sections

| # | Section | Its job | Beliefs it earns | Also called |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Promise | The outcome they desire | 1 | Promise of Outcome |
| 2 | Problem | What's causing the desire for change | 2, 5 | Problem at Stake |
| 3 | Pain Points | Relatable symptoms | 2 | |
| 4 | Proof of Results | Why you're a credible source | 4 | |
| 5 | Personal Epiphany | How you discovered this idea | 4 | |
| 6 | Process to Outcome | The vehicle that gets the result | 3, 6 | Process to Solution |
| 7 | Positioning of Offer | Why it's better than the other options | 3 | |
| 8 | Price Anchor | The no-brainer deal | 8 | |
| 9 | Pricing Tables | Multiple plans, if there are any | 8, 10 | |
| 10 | Product Breakdown | What's inside | 6, 9 | Product BTS Breakdown, What's Inside |
| 11 | Product Bonuses | What else they get | 8 | |
| 12 | Proof from Others | Reviews, testimonials and outside trust | 7 | Proof of Recommendations |
| 13 | Perfect Persona | Who it's for and who it's not for | 1 | |
| 14 | Project Spotlight | Case studies | 7 | |
| 15 | Pushback Reflex | Risk reversal: the guarantee | 10 | |
| 16 | Priority | Why it's important to act now | 12 | |
| 17 | Promotional Bar | The announcement bar at the top and the final call to action at the bottom | 11 | |
| Extra | FAQ and footer | Next steps, access, delivery, refunds, and every objection left | 9, 10, 11 | |

The belief numbers match the twelve beliefs in `brain/CORE.md`. Two jobs ride along with other sections: say why you're making the offer at this price (a short note near the price, so there's no catch), and spell out what happens after they buy (in the FAQ and under the final button).

The numbers are the default sequence. Section order is a split-test variable, so once a page has traffic, test it. A common change on pages with one price: put Product Breakdown and Product Bonuses right before the Price Anchor, so the price lands after the value.

## Which sections each page needs

| Page | Sections |
|---|---|
| Opt-in or lead page | Promotional Bar, Promise, Pain Points, Product Breakdown (what's in the freebie), Proof of Results, call to action |
| Webinar or challenge registration | Promotional Bar, Promise, Pain Points, Product Breakdown (what they'll learn), Proof of Results, Priority (the date) |
| Low-ticket page ($7 to $47) | Promotional Bar, Promise, Proof of Results, Personal Epiphany, Positioning of Offer (before and after), Product Breakdown, Product Bonuses, Price Anchor, Proof from Others, Pushback Reflex, FAQ, Priority |
| Automated or mid-ticket page ($100 to $1,000) | All 17, with Pricing Tables only when there are plans |
| High-ticket or application page | Promise, Problem, Pain Points, Proof of Results, Personal Epiphany, Process to Outcome, Perfect Persona, Project Spotlight, Proof from Others, Priority, with an application call to action. Usually no price on the page |
| Upsell or one-time offer | Promise, Product Breakdown, Price Anchor, Pushback Reflex, Priority |
| Home page | Promise, Proof of Results, Process to Outcome, Perfect Persona, then the freebie and the offers |

Each page type runs on a different pair:

- **Opt-in pages** run on intrigue and desire.
- **Sales pages** run on trust and priority.
- **Applications** run on authority and exclusivity.

## Write a full sales page

1. Read the Brain: the avatar's words (section 3 and the market research grid), the offer, the proof, the objections and the Voice card.
2. Pick the page type above and list the sections you'll use. Write one line per cut section on why.
3. Find the big idea: one new way of seeing the problem that the whole page argues for. Every section holds that frame.
4. Write the hero. The headline is the strongest angle from the research, under 12 words. The subheadline handles the biggest objection ("even if ...").
5. Write each section in order, in the buyer's words. Speak to "you". Short paragraphs, one idea each, bullets for anything that's a list.
6. Calls to action describe the outcome ("Get instant access", "Save my seat", "Apply now"). Put one every one to two scroll lengths, with the guarantee or a reassurance line under each.
7. Proof only from the Brain. With no proof, leave Proof from Others and Project Spotlight out, or use `[TESTIMONIAL: what kind]` slots. Never invent a testimonial, number or name.
8. Run the [conversions-framework](../conversions-framework/SKILL.md) check and the belief audit in [purchase-decision-journey](../purchase-decision-journey/SKILL.md).
9. Run the scanner from [anti-ai-slop-writing](../anti-ai-slop-writing/SKILL.md), fix every HARD finding, then hand over.

## Headline starters

Fill the blanks with the avatar's words and real numbers only.

| Section | Starters |
|---|---|
| Promise | How to ___ without ___. How to ___ so you can ___. How to ___ in ___ without ___. |
| Problem | The truth about ___. The ___ mistakes ___ make. Don't try to ___ before you ___. |
| Pain Points | Does this sound like you? You want more ___, but ___. You're not sure how to ___. |
| Proof of Results | Case study: how ___ got ___. See what happened when ___. |
| Process to Outcome | Introducing the ___ method. Get started in ___ simple steps. |
| Positioning of Offer | The old way vs the new way. The last ___ you'll need. Done for you, start to finish. |
| Perfect Persona | This is for you if ___. This is not for you if ___. This works even if ___. |
| Price Anchor | Total value ___. Normally ___, today ___ (only when the normal price is real). |
| Priority | Price goes up on ___. Only ___ spots. Bonuses end on ___. (All only when true.) |

Headlines come in two families: gain (how to get the outcome, the method, the steps) and pain (the mistake, the warning, what it's costing them). Test one of each.
