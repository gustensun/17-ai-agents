# DM closing scripts

How to close clients by message, without pressure and often without a call. The prospect reaches the decision themselves: you help them get clear, then make the next step easy. The ready-to-adapt messages are in [references/scripts.md](references/scripts.md).

## Why people buy from a stranger online

Trust. And a busy buyer checks three things before they trust you. Think about hiring a designer: you ask to see examples, you ask what they charge, and you ask how many they can do per week.

1. **Proof:** the work does the selling.
2. **Pricing:** it fits what they planned to spend.
3. **Availability:** how much you can get done in their timeframe.

Everything else is gut feeling. An offer with no risk and no effort ("I'll redesign one section of your page, you don't need to send me anything") is an easy yes. When your work is clearly great, a serious buyer goes straight to price and payment.

Keep messages short and to the point. Confident, never cocky. Friendly, relaxed and detached from the outcome.

## Six mistakes that cost sales

1. **Too excited.** Nobody wants to buy from a fan. Stay neutral. They need you as much as you need them.
2. **Slow or lazy replies.** The faster you move the conversation forward, the better your chances. Keep following up when timing is slow: leads come back weeks later.
3. **Too many questions.** Questions that don't affect the work make you look like you don't operate from authority. Ask what's relevant.
4. **Too few questions.** They need to feel you have what you need to do the job well.
5. **No follow-up.** People are busy and overwhelmed. Be helpful, kind and ready when they are.
6. **Entitled.** Clients don't owe you a reply. Be the person who gives and helps, never the one who takes or gets passive-aggressive.

## The 3C's

**Connect.** Start a conversation before any pitch. When someone posts that they need help and the comments fill up with "DM me, I can help", offer something useful instead: your portfolio, an example close to what they need. End with a question, so you're the one comment that invites a reply. Then send a few short messages, never one long one. Wait for them to talk. No chasing within the first day.

**Collect.** Ask before you price, like a doctor who examines before prescribing. Most leads ask for the price early. Answer with what it depends on, and collect what you need: what they have, what's missing, the offer, the price point, the customer, the software, the proof, the timeline. Mirror back what they said they want. If you're fully booked, you can give the price up front to save both of you time (the qualifier filter).

**Close.** Give a clear price with an anchor (the normal price for the full scope, then this price for what they need), and the timeline. Then wait. When they ask "what are the next steps?", use the clarity closer: you send the invoice, then an onboarding form that collects everything, so they can focus on other things while you build. Clear next steps close the sale. Mirror their words, and only sell what you can deliver: if you can't help, say so.

## Rules for the agents

- **The owner sends.** The Sales agent and the Copywriter draft. Every message to a person waits for the owner's yes (house rule 2), unless a standing rule in `my-business/team/sales.md` says otherwise.
- **Never copy and paste the same message to many people.** Platforms flag it as spam and can restrict the account, and people can tell. Every message mentions something specific about that person.
- **Use the owner's voice** from the Voice card, and the owner's real prices, timelines and proof from the Brain. Missing facts stay in brackets.
- **The onboarding form** can be the [40 context questions](../../setup/40-context-questions.md), trimmed to what the project needs, so the client's Brain gets built during onboarding.
- **Follow the platform's rules** for messages, groups and posts. Some platforms show fewer posts that ask for comments. If reach drops, ask people to send a message instead.

## Which script when

| Situation | Script in references/scripts.md |
|---|---|
| Someone posts that they need help | 1. The comment |
| Cold outreach to a prospect | 2. Outbound connect, with 4 follow-ups |
| They replied and want to know more | 3. Collect: paid or free |
| They said yes | 4. Close: invoice and onboarding |
| They messaged you first | 5. Inbound connect, collect, close |
| They went quiet, or price is the issue | 6. Follow-ups |
| Before you give a price | 7. Questions before closing |
| No clients yet | 8. Your first 10 clients, and 11. The case study post |
| Growing from 10 to 50 clients | 9. Free templates |
| Someone joins your community | 10. The welcome message |
| They want a call | 12. The sales call |

Experts are beginners who spent more time preparing. Before every conversation, read the prospect's site and profile, and have two or three examples from the portfolio ready for their kind of business ("example closing").
