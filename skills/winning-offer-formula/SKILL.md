# Winning Offer Formula

An offer is the bridge from the buyer's current situation to their dream destination. It works when nine elements are clear, grouped in three parts. Sometimes the best offers show people the cost of NOT taking action.

## The nine elements

| Part | Element | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Transformation | Who | Trendy markets with a big audience, purchase power, and available online |
| Transformation | Outcome | The result they want, for example a six figure business, YouTube growth, fat loss, better relationships |
| Transformation | Problem | The pain points showing up in their life because of their current situation |
| Transportation | Vehicle | The process and method you use to get them to the outcome |
| Transportation | Source | Who you are as a person to be able to help them get to the outcome |
| Transportation | Time | How long it takes to get the outcome with your solution |
| Transaction | Fee | The investment required to get your help and the outcome |
| Transaction | Risk | What they're losing or potentially risking by making a purchase decision |
| Transaction | Priority | Why they should act now instead of delaying |

## The 3T Offer Triangle

Each pair of parts creates one feeling the buyer needs:

- **Transformation + Transportation = Compulsion.** Desire: they want it and see how they'd get there.
- **Transformation + Transaction = Confidence.** Trust: the result is worth the fee and the risk.
- **Transaction + Transportation = Convenience.** Ease: it's simple to buy and simple to follow.

When an offer stalls, find the missing feeling. Wanting it but not buying usually means confidence is missing. Buying and not finishing usually means convenience is missing.

## Objection handling offer hacks

Turn concerns, fears and objections into sales by building the answer into the offer itself.

| Type | What they say | Build into the offer |
|---|---|---|
| Product-based | "Are funnels for my offer?" "Do funnels work for ecom?" "Which software do I need?" "I'm not sure keto is legit." "Ads don't work anymore." "Supplements are a scam." | Examples from their exact situation, a tool-neutral setup, proof of the method, a comparison with what they tried |
| People-based | "I don't feel confident in this." "I don't have an eye for design." "I'm not a techy person." "The last course was crappy." "I've wasted money before." "Nothing's worked before." | Done-for-you templates, step-by-step walkthroughs, support, a guarantee, a quick first win |
| Priority-based | "I'm so busy already with life." "I don't have a budget for this." "I have bills coming up." "I told my partner I wouldn't." "Do I really have the energy?" "I'm already so focused on this." | A smaller time commitment, a lower entry price or payment plan, the cost of waiting in numbers, a format that fits their week |

Use the objections from Brain section 8 (Q27 to Q30). Each objection gets one element of the offer, one line on the sales page, and one FAQ answer.

## Build an offer

1. Fill the nine elements in the Brain's Winning Offer Formula table from the interview: Who (Q23), Outcome (Q12), Problem (Q10), Vehicle (Q19), Source (Q15, Q18), Time (Q29), Fee (Q9), Risk (Q30, Q31), Priority (Q33, Q34).
2. Check the triangle: write one sentence each for compulsion, confidence and convenience. Any sentence you can't write is the gap to fix.
3. Match each top objection to an offer element (bonus, guarantee, format, payment option, support).
4. Place the offer on the value ladder ([seven-figure-math](../seven-figure-math/SKILL.md)) and check the price against the break-even math.
5. Write the one-page offer brief below and send it to the owner for approval.

```
OFFER BRIEF: [offer name]
Who / Outcome / Problem: [one line each]
Vehicle / Source / Time: [one line each]
Fee / Risk / Priority: [one line each]
Compulsion: [sentence]   Confidence: [sentence]   Convenience: [sentence]
Objections answered: [objection -> offer element]
Ladder: [step before] -> this offer -> [step after]
Placeholders to fill: [list]
```

## Rules

- Priority must be real: a true deadline, a real seat limit, a real price change, or the honest cost of waiting.
- Time promises need proof. Without it, say what a typical first week looks like instead.
- Price changes and new offers always need the owner's approval.
